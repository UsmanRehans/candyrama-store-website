# Avery: website video evidence and test brief

September 21, 2026. Internal recommendation; no implementation or publication. Read against the latest owner request for wider, beautiful, easygoing scenes with recognizable Candy Rama packaging and brand name.

## Recommendation and evidence strength

Start with one eight-second, silent, locked-camera lifestyle scene, complete pink pouch visible throughout, gentle ambient movement, and a useful still poster from the same art. Preserve the prior plan's user-initiated playback: poster plus Play is the first pilot. Vita recommends 16:9 desktop and separately composed 4:3 mobile. These durations and ratios are creative proposals, not performance findings. Review legibility and full packaging at actual phone size before generation proceeds beyond the first scene.

Place the candidate after the complete product lineup, before the existing brand story, within the catalog's width and existing soft visual treatment. Preserve the three-pouch hero, header, toolbar and product ordering. This follows the owner's recorded requirement that products remain near the top; it is not evidence that this placement increases sales. Avoid a carousel or several simultaneous films in the first test.

Current `app/page.tsx` has hero → all products → Small team story → cravings → Pick Four. Replacing the story image would avoid extra page depth, as Lauren noted, but attaching a generated lifestyle scene directly to factual team copy could imply documentary footage. A distinct campaign panel with a persistent AI disclosure better separates its purpose. Vita and Lauren converged on this placement; Lauren proposes the concise visible caption “AI-created brand scene.” Keep internal assets additionally marked as concepts. Cando should resolve the extra-scroll tradeoff against the rendered page, not an assumed conversion benefit. A future compact reuse of the Pick Four media slot is another option, but fewer visitors may encounter a lower placement; that is a hypothesis to measure.

## Playback and loading contract

- Show the reviewed poster immediately when the module is encountered. Give the slot a fixed aspect ratio so switching to video does not change its size. Branding must work without playback.
- First pilot: attach video sources only on Play, then play muted and inline. `preload="none"` alone is only a hint, and adding `autoplay` can trigger offscreen downloads. Remove the audio stream from exports. Use a native video element with verified codec support and an MP4 fallback rather than a third-party player. These choices follow [web.dev's video performance guidance](https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance), checked September 21, 2026.
- Optional later enhancement, subject to its own evaluation: start subtle motion on viewport entry only when motion is permitted. This is an explicit alternative to the current click-to-play boundary, not a prerequisite for an easygoing visual feel. Retain source gating, poster fallback and all pause protections.
- Provide a visible, keyboard-accessible Pause/Play control with an accurate accessible name. Pause offscreen and when the document is hidden; never undo a visitor's manual pause on reentry. Keep the poster and control usable when autoplay is rejected or media fails.
- Automatically moving content lasting more than five seconds beside other content requires a pause, stop or hide mechanism unless essential. A repeated short loop does not make persistent motion harmless. Source: [W3C Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide), checked September 21, 2026.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, show only the poster by default and offer deliberate Play; do not fetch video until requested. Respond to preference changes and keep Pause available. This is a conservative product choice consistent with [web.dev accessible motion guidance](https://web.dev/learn/accessibility/motion), checked September 21, 2026.
- Essential words and links belong in HTML. Avoid narration and unique information delivered only by moving imagery in this first scene. Any later meaningful audio/story needs its own accessible alternatives. Caption-free silent ambience must not become a loophole for inaccessible information.

## Smallest useful test

First conduct a local side-by-side review of the same scene as poster and video. Ask a few reviewers to name the brand, identify the product being offered, find a product to shop, and explain whether they think the scene depicts actual customers or operations. Report actual observations and counts; a small review is diagnostic, not a population estimate. Reject package distortion, illegible brand lettering, misleading documentary interpretation, or distracting motion before testing engagement.

Check phone and desktop crops; keyboard control/focus; reduced motion on initial load and mid-session; offscreen and hidden-tab pause; manual pause persistence; slow network; media errors; and loop seam. Test rejected autoplay only if evaluating that later variant. Use network inspection to verify no video request before Play and no initial hero/product download competition. Compare loading, layout stability and interaction responsiveness against the same build with a static poster. No performance measurements have yet been taken for this candidate. Initial project goals, not universal standards: mobile movie at most 2 MB, desktop movie at most 4 MB, poster at most 150 KB, and zero layout shift from media replacement. Validate whether these budgets preserve the package lettering; reduce unnecessary scene complexity before sacrificing identity.

If publication is later authorized and traffic supports an experiment, compare poster-only versus the same scene with motion; keep placement, copy, dimensions, products and offers constant. Randomize at session level and report dates, timezone, device mix, counts and uncertainty. Set duration/sample requirements after obtaining baseline traffic and minimum meaningful effect; do not declare a winner from a few sessions.

Proposed event definitions: panel exposure = at least 50% visible for one continuous second; play mode = automatic or user initiated; manual pause; playback error; subsequent product view; add-to-cart success. Exposure threshold is an instrumentation convention, not an industry benchmark. Use eligible assigned sessions as the main comparison denominator; exposure-only comparisons can be biased if the treatment changes who scrolls. Report exposed sessions separately as a diagnostic.

The immediate quality measure is correct brand/product recognition without confusion about authenticity. A later commercial primary metric could be product-detail visits per assigned homepage session, with add-to-cart success and paid-order conversion as separate downstream outcomes. Distinguish paid orders from unpaid order requests. Video plays, loops and completion are diagnostics rather than proof of trust or commercial success. Track page-load/interaction regressions, errors, and product-finding failures as guardrails.
