# Lauren: calm website video direction

September 21, 2026. Editorial recommendation for the latest owner correction. No render, storefront change or publication approval is represented by this document.

## Recommendation

Make one beautiful, wider Candy Rama scene first. The pouch belongs naturally on a table alongside a modest bowl of the matching candy. Keep the complete pouch and its wordmark visible from the poster frame through the final frame. The product name should be learned from the packaging, without a giant floating logo, advertising end card or campaign headline. Space, warm light and small movement can carry the mood. Avoid a sequence of close-up candy shots.

Place the first preview in one standalone media figure immediately after the full product lineup. Preserve the existing three-pouch hero, navigation, toolbar, catalog and story. Give this figure no new heading, paragraph or shopping button. The existing page already provides those functions. Use the site's soft corners and restrained spacing rather than a new campaign design system. This adds one media block; compare it against the page without the block before choosing the final site treatment.

Suggested visible caption, outside the footage: **AI-created brand scene.** In an internal concepts gallery, use **AI-generated promotional concept.** Keep the visitor-facing label readable and persistent, including on the poster. It should explain the imagery without becoming the creative headline. Do not imply that this is real customer footage, the Candy Rama team, packing operations or evidence of product quality.

Suggested controls: **Play video**, **Pause video**. Final accessible description must describe the actual accepted output; provisional wording is “AI-created Candy Rama scene with a pink pouch and a bowl of candy on a sunny table.” Do not use this wording if the final scene differs.

## Why this placement

The current `app/page.tsx` places the entire catalog before `discovery-story`. Its story headline is “Small team. Big candy curiosity.” and its link is “Meet Candy Rama.” Replacing that image with an AI clip would reuse space efficiently, but places invented imagery next to factual team storytelling. A separate figure makes its promotional role clearer and preserves the owner's brand-story priority. No available evidence establishes that this placement improves conversion.

I initially proposed using the existing story image slot. Avery recommended separating campaign imagery from team statements and real reviews; Vita independently flagged the same adjacency concern. The revised recommendation favors clear separation, with one added block as the explicit tradeoff. If the full-page preview becomes too long, a later alternative is replacing the existing movie-night image in the Pick Four section, provided its composition supports that occasion and the label remains visible. Do not create several video sections to use every render.

## Copy and content boundaries

- No extra on-video copy. Preserve the approved on-pack Candy Rama artwork and “Taste the Twist” signature exactly.
- No invented new flavors, origin claims, handmade/hand-packed language, quality guarantees or customer reactions.
- A sunny tabletop can suggest an easy candy break without asserting an actual customer occasion. Real brand/team stories and verified reviews have separate evidentiary jobs.
- Keep all reviews out of the concept footage. Stars, review quotes, customer counts and “loved by” claims require genuine sourced evidence and belong in their own review presentation.
- The site's verified marketplace footer remains a separate functional priority; a platform logo must not appear merely because it is present on a reference site.

Vita checks the exact generation prompt and references, then every output and final crop.
