# Candy Rama: calm website video pilot

September 21, 2026. Cando synthesis of actual Vita, Lauren and Avery collaboration. Direction for the next internal prototype; no new render, storefront implementation or deployment.

## Decision

Make **one beautiful, easygoing scene with naturally visible packaging**, not three portrait ads. Use the complete pink pouch and its real artwork to identify Candy Rama. Keep the camera wide and steady, allow room around the objects, and let subtle light movement supply the motion. No giant candy macro, floating title, package entrance, quick-cut sequence or closing advertisement card.

This follows the owner's latest correction. The three generated initial studies remain locally available as experiments, but they are not approved by the owner as the website direction. The prior proposed punchy branded edits were not rendered and are superseded.

## The first scene

A warm cream tabletop with one complete pink rainbow pouch slightly right of center and a small matching candy dish forward-left. Soft pink background and restrained yellow accent. The package occupies roughly one-third of the desktop frame height; enough to read the name without filling the view. Eight seconds, one locked shot, diffuse light movement, no people or implied real packing/team activity.

Use unchanged existing packaging artwork, matched perspective and an actual contact shadow. The background must support that static package instead of moving behind it inconsistently. Review the first and last frames before calling any loop seamless. Existing generated candy references are concept art, not proof of exact SKU contents.

Desktop: 16:9. Mobile: a separately composed 4:3 scene with package and dish closer together, complete silhouettes and readable branding. Do not crop the desktop master into a narrow vertical ad. Produce a matching poster for each layout.

## Where it belongs

Preserve header/toolbar → three-pouch hero → complete product lineup. Prototype the scene immediately after the lineup and before the existing team story, at the catalog's content width with the established soft edge treatment.

Lauren recommends no new headline, paragraph or CTA: the scene is a visual pause in the shopping page. Use one readable outside-frame caption, **“AI-created brand scene.”** Keep that label present on the poster too. This separates promotional illustration from genuine customer reviews and real business stories. The tradeoff is one added page block; inspect whole-page rhythm before committing to the placement. No evidence currently proves a sales benefit.

## How it behaves

First pilot: poster plus **Play video**, muted inline playback, visible **Pause video**, and no video request until Play. Preserve dimensions during playback. Pause when offscreen or the tab is hidden, and respect a visitor's manual pause. Reduced-motion users see the poster unless they deliberately play. A future ambient-autoplay treatment is a separate option to evaluate, not the default decision.

Keep essentials in HTML, not in moving imagery. Avoid third-party embeds and simultaneous competing videos. Avery's provisional export goals are 2 MB mobile, 4 MB desktop and 150 KB posters; verify real image quality and lettering rather than treating these as universal limits.
