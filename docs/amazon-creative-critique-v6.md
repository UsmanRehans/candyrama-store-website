## The gap

Revision 05 is a clean packaging presentation, not yet a persuasive Amazon merchandising story. My previous live QA pass checked readable copy, consistent identity, and honest review labels. It did not establish that the creative answers enough shopper questions or earns attention. That distinction should have been made more prominently.

The A+ sequence uses the same pouch, a crop of its window, and the pouch again. Three panels deliver roughly one message: colorful candy in a pink bag. The main-character line carries personality; the next two slogans add little buying information. The close-up is through plastic rather than a tactile view of the candy itself. Color is visible without explanatory copy. The third pouch largely repeats the first.

The Store promises a collection but leads to one gummy concept. It does not help someone choose between the two visible varieties. Repeating the same supporting sentence in hero and feature wastes a chance to explain a difference. The generous peach space is orderly, but the sequence needs stronger appetite and discovery, not more blank space or more slogans.

Keep the simplicity. Improve what each panel teaches. Do not equate polished packaging, bug-free rendering, or correct voice with persuasive creative; none proves conversion.

## Stronger sequence, with facts to obtain

These are message jobs and conditional writing scaffolds, not ready-to-publish product claims. Bracketed fields belong in the internal brief only.

| Module | Shopper question and proposed headline | Useful content | Smallest evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Product hero | What exactly is it? **CANDY WITH MAIN CHARACTER ENERGY.** | Add the approved product name and a single clear descriptor: `[product name] — [accurate candy type]`. | 2. Taste and texture | What will it taste and feel like? **MEET YOUR NEXT CRAVING.** | Two or three actual components, each with a concise verified flavor/texture line. | 4. Occasion, optional | Where does this fit? **PRESS PLAY. PASS THE CANDY.** | A recognizable, plausible sharing scene with the same actual product. Short invitation rather than quantity/value claims. | Actual product imagery and portion styling that does not imply one bag fills an excessive bowl. |
| 5. Range comparison, only when real | Which one suits me? **PICK YOUR CRAVING.** | Compare available Candy Rama choices on verified candy type, flavor/texture, and pack format. Three useful modules are better than five repetitive ones. Start with identity, taste/texture, and exact purchase contents. Add a sharing scene or comparison only when it earns its space. Keep Taste the Twist as a signature, not an entire repeated product-information panel.
