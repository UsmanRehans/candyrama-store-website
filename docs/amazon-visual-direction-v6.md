# Candy Rama visual direction v6

Vita · September 13, 2026 · Storyboard and critique only; no new assets generated or deployed.

## What revision 5 actually achieved

I re-inspected the captured live Store and A+ pages and read the current brand, workflow, role and Avery evidence brief. Revision 5 cleaned up packaging, lettering and responsive presentation. My prior pass correctly covered those defects, but the quality gate was too narrow for the owner's creative goal. I should also have challenged whether the sequence made candy desirable and helped a shopper choose. A technically sound asset proof is not a persuasive Amazon campaign. I own that gap.

The A+ sequence repeats one image three ways: pouch, crop through its window, smaller pouch. The shopper learns almost nothing new after the first panel. The detail image magnifies a plastic-window view rather than giving a satisfying, deliberate view of candy texture. Its headline describes color already visible in the image. The large empty peach area was reserved for composition but remains empty while the headline sits below, spending attention without conveying anything. There is no flavor guide, useful pack information, credible serving scene or choice aid.

The Store has a range header but only one discovery tile. “What sounds good?” and “Explore the collection” promise a choice that the layout does not deliver. Cream and peach make the pink legible, but repeated rectangular product shots create a showroom mood. Candy Rama's bold copy needs an equally appetizing focal point and a more purposeful sequence.

## Evidence boundary

The earlier `amazon-team-evidence.md` cites official brand websites, not an inspected set of actual Amazon A+ creatives. It supports the need for specific product information and assortment clarity; it cannot support saying our Amazon layouts compare well against competitors. Avery is researching actual Amazon pages on this assignment. Do not call this document an Amazon competitor visual audit before that evidence arrives. No creative treatment here is proven to increase conversion.

Lauren independently identified the same problem and sent a direct recommendation: product identity, approved flavor/texture guide with candy out of the bag, exact pack facts, then an optional credible occasion. We agree that more decorations or more repetitive modules will not solve it.

## Concrete A+ storyboard — four core modules, fifth only if useful

Module mapping is a working plan using the existing Miles specification, subject to final account/module verification. Keep all meaningful text editable. One image job and one shopper question per module.

Do not default to six modules. Four useful modules are enough for this story; missing evidence should reduce the final count rather than produce fake specificity. A complete content plan can still show internal wireframe annotations for facts and photographs awaiting approval, clearly outside shopper-facing artwork.

## Store storyboard

1. A compact brand header preserves recognizable pink range packaging. Do not make the 5:1 canvas carry the full sales story.
2. Follow with one strong candy-and-pouch feature composition, a short identity-led headline, and an action that leads to an actual matching collection.
3. Present real choices in two or three equally sized product/collection tiles. Each has its own accurate pouch, visible candy and useful distinguishing line. If only one product is confirmed, use a single product feature and remove the language promising a range.
4. Add one restrained occasion scene if it contributes meaning, then a real product destination. Avoid repeating the same image just to fill vertical space.

## Visual rules for the next round

- Keep the pink pouch and established plum/pink/yellow/cream identity; use peach as a supporting photographic surface, not the entire visual idea.
- Alternate a full product view, a tactile close-up, a factual diagram and a human scene. Each must add information or a distinct reason to care.
- Put appetite in actual candy lighting and texture. Avoid a packshot-only story and generic generated bears, rings or cherries.
- Use a maximum of one principal message per panel. Native body copy explains the image; it should not merely repeat its headline.
- Inspect the full sequence as small thumbnails and at mobile size. If two adjacent panels do the same job, merge or replace one.
- Review exact prompts before generation, actual outputs afterward, then finished artwork and live layouts. Keep this existing fidelity gate and add the creative-story gate below.

## Creative-story gate before production

For every module, Vita and Lauren must state its shopper question, new information, image evidence and place in the sequence. Cando should reject a visually clean panel if it has no answer. Review actual competitor Amazon screenshots beside the storyboard once Avery supplies them, distinguishing observed modules from inference. The team should be able to explain why this image makes the specific candy appetizing or easier to choose without relying on “it looks polished.”

Required next inputs for product-accurate execution: selected SKU/ASIN, approved flat label, clear photographs of its loose candy and confirmed flavor/texture/pack facts. These are source needs, not a request to stop concept development. First deliverable should be the annotated storyboard with real reference evidence, before spending effort on another set of near-identical pack renders.
