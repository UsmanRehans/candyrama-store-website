# Candy Rama brand voice

Canonical editorial reference · September 13, 2026 · Lauren maintains this file.

## Source and precedence

The owner asked the team to preserve the established website voice. This reference captures the live homepage, About page, and three linked product pages read on September 13, 2026. [Source record](../brand-voice-source-2026-09-13.md) distinguishes live copy, local additions, and unverified claims. Latest explicit owner corrections take precedence; this is a voice reference, not a substantiation record.

## Personality

Friendly, loud, playful, confident, and candy-obsessed. The brand sounds like a welcoming friend bringing the fun to the table. Everyday American language, a little cheek, and vivid candy descriptions. Texas warmth belongs in the personality; exaggerated regional mannerisms do not. Accessible and sociable, never luxury-exclusive or corporate. The energy is bolder than generic gentle happiness copy.

## Rhythm and vocabulary

Short punchy headlines, often two or three clipped beats. Follow the energetic headline with a plain sentence that tells the shopper something useful. Use direct invitations and active verbs: pick, pass, bring, taste, share. Favor candy, craving, stash, flavor, color, drama, movie night; use sour, tangy, chewy, crunchy, or kick only when the product actually supports them. One playful idea per block is enough. Avoid a string of slang in every sentence.

Established homepage anchors: “Candy with main character energy.” “Pick your craving.” “More candy. More drama.” These establish confidence, directness, and playful exaggeration. [Live homepage](https://candyrama-store.vercel.app/).

The About page emphasizes social occasions, familiar classics alongside new flavors, and sensory contrasts; its social phrase is “Made for passing around.” [Live About page](https://candyrama-store.vercel.app/about).

Product descriptions use a compact flavor plus sensory finish, as in blue raspberry described with a puckery finish. This pattern is useful; the catalog text does not establish an Amazon SKU’s contents. [Blue Raspberry Blast](https://candyrama-store.vercel.app/product/blue-raspberry-blast).

## Writing rules

- Lead with the candy experience, then the useful detail, then the action.
- Keep a bold headline paired with calm, readable supporting text.
- Make the invitation specific to the scene: sharing at a movie night, choosing a craving, reaching for a treat.
- Keep packaging recognizable, but never make a packaging transition the campaign story.
- Treat “Taste the Twist” as the packaging signature; preserve exact approved artwork.
- Use Candy Rama in new prose, matching the owner/team naming and stacked wordmark. The deployed website uses CandyRama; this recorded inconsistency is not authorization for a site-wide rename.
- Do not import checkout deals, prices, badges, availability, or website service promises into Amazon creative.
- Do not substitute upscale jargon, wellness language, unsupported superiority, or an invented artisan/manufacturing story for the established voice.

## Image and headline fit

The image must earn the headline. A bold candy headline needs an appetizing, legible product focal point; the scene can stay restrained and peach/cream so the pink pouch supplies the energy. A movie-night headline belongs beside a plausible sharing scene. A gummy detail panel must show the actual gummy product, not fruit slices or an invented mix. Never infer flavor from color alone.

Lauren checks copy and image together for identity, energy, readable hierarchy, content match, supported claims, and mobile cropping. Vita checks exact packaging fidelity and production finish, including the latest owner direction for a smooth filled pouch. Neither a nice image nor approved tone verifies ingredients or manufacturing.

## Current Amazon direction

The earlier “Happiness, by the handful” draft fits the category but softens Candy Rama’s established personality. Replace it as the principal hero; it may remain secondary language if needed. Proposed three-module copy:

1. **CANDY WITH MAIN CHARACTER ENERGY.** — Make room for your next candy craving.
2. **BIG CANDY ENERGY.** — Take a closer look at your next sweet pick.
3. **PRESS PLAY. PASS THE CANDY.** — Set out a bowl, find your spot, and make a night of it.

The second headline deliberately avoids a flavor/texture claim until the target SKU is verified. Once facts are approved, replace its generic supporting line with one precise flavor-and-texture sentence. Additional detail modules should have distinct informational jobs, not repeat the same energy claim. A closing pouch panel can use **TASTE THE TWIST.**

## Claims are a separate review

The live site contains manufacturing, hand-packing, workshop, small-batch, bestseller, and promotional language. Their presence establishes that the copy is published, not that the claims are verified. Do not transfer them to new assets without evidence. Texas-based is recorded owner direction; it does not imply Texas-made. Local-made and affordability remain ambitions until substantiated. Keep origin, ingredients, allergens, certifications, net weight, contents, inventory, and comparative claims tied to approved product/business records.

## Latest owner direction — September 14, 2026

Prioritize the small Texas business's curiosity, candy variety, and enthusiasm for bringing new discoveries to online customers. Use fewer words and let product selection and imagery carry more of the story. Earlier “main character energy” and “drama” examples are historical voice references, not mandatory lead messages. Sound personal, eager, straightforward, and candy-obsessed; avoid corporate innovation language and exaggerated Texas mannerisms.

Suggested direction, not yet approved campaign copy: “Small Texas team. Big candy curiosity.” Use specific discovery invitations such as “See what's new” when linked to genuine current arrivals. Show breadth using actual available products. Fast development of new offerings must not become an unsupported shipping-speed promise or a guaranteed release cadence.

## Owner decision — September 14, 2026: typography direction

The owner selected the second typography concept: Bricolage Grotesque for expressive headlines paired with DM Sans for product names, prices, navigation, controls, and body copy. Treat this as the approved direction for the next Candy Rama website design pass. It does not by itself authorize publication or require the display face on dense legal or administrative surfaces.

## Owner direction — September 15, 2026: let the candy talk

Keep the homepage hero visually led by candy and the pink Candy Rama packaging. Use very little hero copy. Text baked into hero imagery is limited to the Candy Rama name, logo, or “Taste the Twist” slogan. Put longer brand explanations, health and safety guidance, dietary information, and policies on their own pages where visitors intentionally seek detail.
