# CandyRama Voice Guide

## The voice

CandyRama sounds bold, joyful, warm, and a little dramatic.

We talk like the friend who brought the best candy to the party and already knows everyone will ask where it came from. We are playful without trying too hard. We are confident without sounding corporate. We make every interaction feel colorful, human, and delicious.

Our writing should make people want to taste something.

## The golden rule

Never use hyphens, en dashes, or em dashes in customer facing copy.

Use a period, comma, colon, or a new sentence instead.

Write small batch, not the joined version.

Write customer care, not the joined version.

Write gluten free, dairy free, and nut free as open phrases.

Rewrite any sentence that appears to need a dash. Shorter sentences usually sound more like CandyRama anyway.

## Our personality

### Bold

Lead with flavor, feeling, or a clear promise. Do not hide the good part.

Say: Big berry flavor with a sour little plot twist.

Avoid: This product features a berry flavor profile with sour notes.

### Joyful

Candy should feel fun. Use energetic words, surprising details, and a sense of delight.

Say: Open the bag. Start the party.

Avoid: Open package before consuming.

### Warm

Sound like a real person who cares. Be direct, helpful, and generous.

Say: Something went wrong. Give it another try, or send us a message and we will help.

Avoid: An unknown error has occurred.

### Dramatic

A little drama is part of the name. Use it for flavor and excitement, never for pressure or fear.

Say: Sweet at first. Sour by surprise. Gone before you know it.

Avoid: Buy now before it is too late.

## How we write

1. Keep sentences short and easy to say out loud.

2. Use active verbs such as grab, taste, share, crunch, twist, pour, and discover.

3. Put the tastiest detail first.

4. Use contractions when they sound natural.

5. Choose plain words over business language.

6. Use one strong joke or playful line instead of filling every sentence with jokes.

7. Be specific about flavors, textures, sizes, ingredients, timing, and policies.

8. Use exclamation points with restraint. One is plenty.

9. Never make health, ingredient, or allergy claims that we cannot verify.

10. Never use guilt, false urgency, or confusing language to push a sale.

## Words we love

Bold. Sweet. Sour. Tangy. Crunchy. Chewy. Gooey. Juicy. Colorful. Wild. Classic. Fresh. Twist. Treat. Craving. Favorite. Drama. Party. Share. Stash. Sprinkle. Bite. Bag. Batch.

## Words we avoid

Premium. Innovative. Solutions. Utilize. Leverage. Synergy. Consumers. Purchase journey. Flavor profile. Mouthfeel. Artisanal. Disruptive. Best in class.

Use premium only when it communicates a specific and provable product fact.

## Brand rhythm

The classic CandyRama rhythm uses contrast and a quick payoff.

Sweet, then sour, then seriously good.

Tiny candy. Huge personality.

More candy. More drama.

Made for sharing. Good luck with that.

The first sentence sets the scene. The second sentence adds the twist.

## Headlines

Headlines should be short, vivid, and easy to remember.

Good examples:

Turn up the taste.

Meet your new favorite candy.

Bring on the sour.

This bag has range.

Candy with main character energy.

Avoid headlines that could belong to any company, such as Quality You Can Trust or Something for Everyone.

## Product descriptions

Start with taste and texture. Follow with the moment or mood. End with useful details.

Example:

Bright blue raspberry flavor with a serious sour sparkle. Chewy, electric, and ready to wake up your candy stash. Packed fresh in Houston and made for movie nights, road trips, and very selective sharing.

Keep claims honest. If a candy is packed rather than made by CandyRama, say packed. If it contains or may contact an allergen, say so clearly and without jokes.

## Buttons and calls to action

Buttons should tell people exactly what happens next. They can still have personality.

Good examples:

Add to bag

Shop the candy

Find your flavor

See all treats

Start your order

Send us a message

Avoid vague labels such as Submit, Continue, Learn More, or Click Here when a clearer action is available.

## Cart and checkout

Keep the fun, but prioritize clarity. Prices, quantities, shipping details, and errors must be easy to understand.

Empty cart example:

Your bag is feeling a little too light. Let us fix that.

Success example:

Order received. The sweet stuff is officially in motion.

Error example:

That payment did not go through. Check your details and try again. Your candy is still waiting for you.

## Customer care

Lead with empathy, then explain the next step. Never joke about a damaged order, a missing package, an allergy, a refund, or a payment problem.

Say:

We are sorry your order arrived damaged. Send us your order number and a photo within 48 hours. We will take it from there.

Avoid:

Oops! Your candy had a rough ride.

## Email and social

Email subject lines should spark curiosity without hiding the point.

Fresh candy just entered the chat

Your sour stash needs this

The crunch everyone keeps talking about

Social copy can be looser, faster, and more playful. Keep the product or idea clear in the first sentence. Do not pile on slang. If a line sounds like a brand pretending to be a teenager, rewrite it.

## Legal, shipping, and allergy copy

Clarity wins. Use direct, calm language. Keep the personality in the heading or introduction, then state the facts plainly.

Do not soften important restrictions. Do not make safety information cute. Never promise an outcome that depends on a carrier, payment provider, or other outside service.

## Capitalization and punctuation

Use sentence case for most headings and interface copy.

Use all caps only for short labels, eyebrows, and intentional moments of emphasis.

Use periods for confidence. Use commas for rhythm. Use colons to introduce useful details.

Do not use hyphens or any dash character.

## Final check

Before publishing, ask:

1. Does this sound like a fun person rather than a company memo?

2. Can the reader understand it on the first pass?

3. Does it make the flavor, action, or next step clear?

4. Is the playfulness appropriate for the moment?

5. Are every claim and product detail accurate?

6. Did any hyphen or dash character sneak in?

If the answer to the last question is yes, rewrite the sentence.
